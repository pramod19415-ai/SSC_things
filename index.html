1. One triangle, six ratios

Pick an angle θ in a right-angled triangle. Slide it and watch: the shape changes, but each ratio is fixed for that angle.

Tap a ratio to light up the sides it uses (hypotenuse = 10 units):

Mnemonic: SOH-CAH-TOA. The flips: cosec = 1/sin, sec = 1/cos, cot = 1/tan. In each pair exactly one name has “co”.

2. Two identities that always hold

Pythagoras: opposite² + adjacent² = hypotenuse². Divide everything by hypotenuse² and you get sin²θ + cos²θ = 1. Divide by adjacent² instead: 1 + tan²θ = sec²θ. By opposite²: 1 + cot²θ = cosec²θ.

3. Complementary angles

The two sharp angles add up to 90°. What is “opposite” for one is “adjacent” for the other, so sin θ = cos(90° − θ), tan θ = cot(90° − θ), sec θ = cosec(90° − θ). “Co-sine” literally means “sine of the complement”.

4. The special angles, rebuilt from two triangles

Read sin, cos, tan straight off these triangles. Or use the counting trick: sin of 0°, 30°, 45°, 60°, 90° is √0, √1, √2, √3, √4 each divided by 2. cos is the same list backwards.

5. Heights and distances, live

You stand d metres from a tower and look up. The height above your eye is h = d × tan θ.

Elevation from you to the top = depression from the top to you (alternate angles). Both are measured from the horizontal.
